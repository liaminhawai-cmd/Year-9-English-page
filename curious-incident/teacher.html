How a session runs

  1. Groups of four or five, each group starting at a different table. Six tables, one prompt each.
  2. Four minutes of talk at the table, then two minutes writing on the session page of the booklet.
  3. Every group moves up one table. Six rotations covers all six prompts in 36 minutes.
  4. Table 1 is the teacher table. You sit there all session; every group comes to you once.
  5. Before the rotations: silent starter (5), Table talk on the screen at level 7, 9 and 11 (8), you modelling it at Table 1 with one group (2).
  6. After (5): two tables read out the one thing that changed someone's mind. Which rubric row?

Timing
60-minute lesson: 5 + 8 + 2 before, 6 × 6 minutes, 5 after. Short on time: drop a rotation, not the pre-teaching.

The booklet

  • One booklet per student, kept for all three sessions. Print it landscape, double-sided, one A4 sheet per two pages, so it is four pages: front page, then one page per session.
  • Front page: name, the continuum rubric, and the Table 1 record that you fill in. Students never write on the front page.
  • Session pages: five rows, one per table (Tables 2 to 6). Table 1 has no row because you score it on the front page. Columns: who was at my table, what my table said, what I think now.
  • Collect booklets at the end of each session if you are scoring that day; otherwise students keep them in their book.
  • Say this once in Session 1. The booklet does not explain it.

Making groups on the shuffler

  1. Open the Group shuffler on the classroom computer, the one that will be on the TV in the lessons. Groups are saved in that browser only.
  2. Paste the class list, one name per line. Six tables and three sessions are already set.
  3. Two students who should not sit together: put them in the keep-apart box as Sam / Alex, one pair per line.
  4. Press Shuffle all sessions. You get six groups for each of the three sessions. Everyone still visits every table; the shuffle changes the company between sessions and keeps repeats low. The repeat count is shown on each session.
  5. To move someone: click their name, then click the group they should be in. Or Edit as text and type the groups by hand. Reshuffle this session tries again for one session only.
  6. Print a session, or leave it. The Prompts page in display mode shows who is at which table for each rotation, from the same saved groups.
  7. Absent students: leave them in the group; a group of three is fine. A student who is away for a whole session simply has a blank page.

What goes on the screen

  • Table talk (Session 1 · Session 2 · Session 3): the worked example. Two students at a table, one line at a time. Next shows a line; Next again marks it against the three rubric rows on the side. Use − and + to change the level: start at 7, then 9, then two lines of 11. The lesson plan for each session says which lines to stop on.
  • Prompts page in display mode (Session 1 · Session 2 · Session 3): pick the session tab, press ▶ Display mode with timer. The six prompts, who is at each table this rotation, and the talk / write clock. Space starts and pauses; the bell sounds at the end of talk and again at the end of writing; then it moves to the next rotation.
  • Rubric (the continuum): for the close of the session, when you ask which row a line lands in.
  • Links with #session-2 on the end open that session straight away. Use them in the lesson plans and on OneNote.

Table 1

  • You sit at Table 1 with the front pages of the booklets of the group in front of you. Listen for four minutes. Do not lead the talk; hand a turn to the quiet one, nothing else.
  • Record on the front page: a level for each strand (Listening, Interacting, Presenting) and the words you heard that put them there. One line each.
  • Level 9 is the Year 9 expected level. Listening 10 to 12 are the draft extension for students working above; use them, they are on the rubric.
  • Presenting is scored from Session 2 (reading the statement and running the talk as Reader) and Session 3 (the sentence to the class).
Listen for

Session 1: a feeling with a because from the words; a question that gets a reason out of someone; a named person answered; who notices the quiet one.

Session 2: a side with a reason from the book; the opponent's exact words repeated before the answer; a reframe the table then uses; a concession that keeps the argument alive; the Reader running it without taking a side.

Session 3: a feature located in words on the page; a "yes, and" that adds; someone's point taken further by someone else; turns given by name; one clear sentence for the class.

Assessment

  • The folio is Assessment Task 1 of the unit. The front page is the record: three sessions, three strands, a level and the evidence for each.
  • The session pages are the student's Listening evidence: what the table said, in their words. Dot points are fine; the evidence is that they heard.
  • Report the highest level shown consistently across the three sessions, not the average. A level 11 line in Session 3 that never appears elsewhere is a note, not a mark.
  • Rubric: the continuum, printable from that page.

EAL and support

  • Pre-teach the sentence starters (bottom of the Prompts page) as a script before Session 1: say each one aloud, students repeat, then use one on a throwaway prompt.
  • Table talk at level 7 is the model for students who need the smallest version of a move first.
  • A minute of first-language planning before the go-round at each table is fine. The talk is in English.
  • Seat EAL students so the Reader role in Session 2 comes to them at a table they have heard once already.
  • A student may write the table's ideas as dot points rather than sentences.
